# Documentation and interface writing

These rules apply to README files, guides, handover documents and interface copy.

- Write for the document's intended reader and task. Use conversation history to establish accurate requirements and facts, not as the outline or a list of topics to mention.
- Before drafting, identify what the reader needs to understand or accomplish. Include a passage only when it serves that purpose. A fact being true, recently discussed or recently implemented does not establish its relevance.
- Review every passage as if the reader has never seen the conversation: would it still deserve space in this type of document? Remove passages whose main purpose is to acknowledge a request, explain a past fix, defend an implementation choice or show that feedback was followed.
- Keep project descriptions focused on purpose, audience, workflow, inputs, outputs and material scope. Ordinary interface behaviour and individual bug fixes belong in task-specific help, tests or change history when useful; do not promote them into headline capabilities.
- Keep operating instructions, maintenance procedures and development history in their appropriate documents. Preserve relevant equipment facts and real limitations, explained at the point where they help the reader act or decide. Do not add assumptions about the reader's biography, a supervisor or circumstances absent from the task.
- Review the complete artifact after editing. Correct the recurring editorial pattern, not just the sentence most recently criticised. Do not claim that a checklist or automated test guarantees editorial relevance.

Do not insert these editorial rules or commentary about following them into reader-facing instructions. They guide the writing process.
